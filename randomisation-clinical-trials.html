Randomisation in Clinical Trials: Methods and Best Practices

Tags Clinical Trials, Biostatistics, Randomisation

Introduction

Randomisation is a cornerstone of clinical trial design, serving as the primary method to eliminate selection bias and ensure the validity of causal inferences. In this post, we'll explore different randomisation techniques and their applications in modern clinical research.

Why Randomisation Matters

The fundamental principle behind randomisation is to create comparable groups that differ only by chance. This ensures that:

  • Known and unknown confounding variables are equally distributed across treatment groups
  • Statistical tests remain valid under their theoretical assumptions
  • The foundation for causal inference is established

Common Randomisation Methods

Simple Randomisation

The most basic form, equivalent to tossing a coin for each participant. While conceptually simple, it may lead to imbalanced group sizes in smaller trials.

Block Randomisation

This method ensures balance by randomly permuting treatment assignments within blocks of predetermined size. It's particularly useful when enrollment occurs over time.

Stratified Randomisation

When prognostic factors are known, stratification ensures balance within important subgroups. This is especially valuable when certain covariates have strong effects on outcomes.

Adaptive Randomisation

Modern approaches that adjust randomisation probabilities based on accrued data, including response-adaptive and covariate-adaptive methods.

Practical Considerations

When implementing randomisation in clinical trials, several factors must be considered:

  • Trial size and expected enrollment rate
  • Importance of maintaining allocation concealment
  • Presence of important prognostic factors
  • Regulatory requirements and statistical analysis plans

Conclusion

Proper randomisation is essential for generating reliable evidence in clinical research. The choice of method should be driven by the specific context of each trial, balancing theoretical considerations with practical implementation challenges.
